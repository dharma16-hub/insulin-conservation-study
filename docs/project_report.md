Comparative Analysis of Insulin Conservation Across Vertebrates


Objective

To investigate the evolutionary conservation of insulin protein sequences across vertebrate species commonly used in biological and diabetes research.



Research Question

How conserved is insulin among vertebrates, and which species possess insulin proteins most similar to humans?



Species Analyzed

Human (Homo sapiens)
Mouse (Mus musculus)
Rat (Rattus norvegicus)
Pig (Sus scrofa)
Cow (Bos taurus)
Zebrafish (Danio rerio)



Methods

Sequence Retrieval
Insulin protein sequences were obtained from the NCBI Protein database and stored in FASTA format.

Multiple Sequence Alignment
Sequences were aligned using Clustal Omega to identify conserved regions and critical amino acid residues.

Pairwise Sequence Alignment
Pairwise comparisons between human insulin and other species were performed using EMBOSS Needle.



Results
Species	Identity (%)
Mouse	    81.8
Rat	      82.7
Pig	      85.5
Cow	      80.0
Zebrafish	40.9



Key Findings
Pig insulin showed the highest similarity to human insulin.
Mammalian insulin sequences displayed strong conservation.
Zebrafish insulin was the most divergent sequence analyzed.
Conserved cysteine residues suggest preservation of insulin structure and function across evolution.



Biological Interpretation
The high degree of conservation among mammalian insulin proteins reflects the essential role of insulin in glucose regulation. Critical cysteine residues responsible for disulfide bond formation remained conserved across all species examined, indicating strong evolutionary pressure to maintain insulin structure and function.



Conclusion
Insulin is highly conserved across vertebrates, particularly among mammals. The observed conservation supports the use of mammalian model organisms in diabetes research and demonstrates how sequence analysis can be used to infer evolutionary and functional relationships.
